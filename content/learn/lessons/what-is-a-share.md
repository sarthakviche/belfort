A **share** is a small piece of ownership in a company. If a company has 100 crore shares and you own 100, you own a tiny slice of its profits, its assets and its debts.

## Why the price moves

The price on your screen is simply the last price at which a buyer and a seller agreed. It moves when more people want to buy than sell, or the other way round. Over **years**, prices tend to follow the company's profits. Over **days and weeks**, they follow news, results, the mood of the whole market, and the flow of money from large investors.

That second fact is why swing and positional trading is possible at all, and also why it is hard: short-term moves are noisy, and nobody knows the next one for certain.

## Where your shares live

- You buy through a **broker**, a firm registered with SEBI that sends your order to the exchange (NSE or BSE).
- Your shares sit in a **demat account** with a depository (NSDL or CDSL), in your name, not the broker's.
- Money and shares change hands one working day after the trade. This is called **T+1 settlement**. The lesson on costs covers it.

> A share price is not a score of how good a company is. A great company can be a poor trade if you pay too much or size the trade badly.

## What this course will and will not do

Belfort teaches a process: read the trend, find a sensible entry, decide where you are wrong, size the trade so a loss is small, and review what happened. It never tells you what to buy. You will practise everything with virtual points first.
