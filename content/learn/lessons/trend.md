A **trend** is the direction price has been travelling. You do not need an indicator to see it. You need **swing points**: the peaks (swing highs) and dips (swing lows) the chart makes as it moves.

## The definition Belfort uses

- **Uptrend**: each rally peak is higher than the last, and each dip is higher than the last. Higher highs, higher lows.
- **Downtrend**: lower highs and lower lows.
- **No clear trend**: peaks and dips are not consistently rising or falling. Price moves sideways in a range.

Belfort also checks where price sits against its moving averages (the average close over 20 and 50 days for swing, 50 and 200 for positional). Price above both averages, with higher highs and lows, is an **established** uptrend.

## New trends are fragile

When price first breaks above its last swing high after a fall, the trend has **turned up, but only recently**. It is confirmed when price pulls back and holds a dip that is higher than the previous one. Many new uptrends fail before that happens, which is why Belfort marks them as early.

## Why trade with the trend

A swing or positional trade needs price to move a few percent in your direction within days or weeks. Buying in an uptrend means the recent behaviour of the stock is on your side. Buying in a downtrend means betting that the selling stops right now, which it often does not. Belfort's research rule excludes downtrends for exactly this reason.
