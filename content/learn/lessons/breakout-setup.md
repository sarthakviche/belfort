A **breakout** happens when price closes above a level it has failed to pass for a while. The idea is that sellers who capped the price there have been absorbed, and new buyers are willing to pay more.

## How Belfort defines it

The research report looks for a close above the highest high of a recent window:

| Style | Window | Volume on the breakout day |
|---|---|---|
| Swing | 20-day high | At least 1.5 times the 20-day average |
| Positional | 55-day high | At least 1.5 times the 20-day average |

Two details matter. It must be a **close** above the level, not just a touch during the day. And price must **still be above** the level when you check; a stock that broke out and fell back has not held the move.

## Why volume decides it

A close above resistance on thin volume means few people took part. Those breakouts often slip back under the level within days. A close on heavy volume shows many buyers acted at the new price. That is why the report labels a breakout on volume below 1.5 times average as "Partly met", with the reason spelled out.

## Planning the trade

1. **Entry:** near the breakout close. Entries far above it make the stop wide.
2. **Stop:** below the breakout level or the last swing low. If price closes back well inside the old range, the idea has failed.
3. **Stretch:** check the "not stretched" line. A stock already more than 3 ATR above its fast average often pulls back before going further.
4. **Room:** a fresh high often has no resistance overhead in the last year, which the report shows as "no resistance level overhead". Plan your exit with a trailing stop instead.
5. **Size:** breakout stops are often wider than pullback stops, so the 1% rule gives fewer shares.

## A worked example

- 20-day high: ₹1,200. Today's close: ₹1,226 on 2.1 times average volume.
- Stop below the breakout level at ₹1,180: ₹46 below entry, about 3.8%.
- Capital ₹5,00,000 at 1% risk is ₹5,000; ₹5,000 ÷ ₹46 is 108 shares.

## Failed breakouts

Many breakouts fail. Price closes above the high, then falls back below it in the next few sessions. The stop limits that loss to about 1R. Expecting failures, and keeping each one small, is part of the setup.
