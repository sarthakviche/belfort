Every trade costs money, even one that makes money. A trader who ignores costs can have a winning process and a losing account.

## What a delivery trade costs

When you buy shares and hold them overnight (a **delivery** trade), the bill usually includes:

- **Brokerage**: zero at many discount brokers for delivery, a percentage at others.
- **Securities Transaction Tax (STT)**: charged by the government on both the buy and the sell.
- **Exchange and SEBI charges**: small percentages of the traded value.
- **Stamp duty**: on the buy side.
- **GST**: on brokerage and exchange charges.
- **Depository charge**: a flat fee per stock on the day you sell, set by your broker.

Exact rates change with budgets and broker plans, so read your broker's charge list and every **contract note** (the bill you get after each trade).

## Why small trades hurt

Flat fees do not shrink with the trade. A ₹15 depository charge is 0.015% of a ₹1,00,000 sale but 1.5% of a ₹1,000 sale. Many tiny trades quietly add up to a large drag. Belfort's practice account deducts a rough percentage on each side, so practice results are not cost-free.

## Taxes on gains

Profits are taxed. Under current rules, gains on shares held for less than a year are **short-term** and taxed at a higher rate than **long-term** gains on shares held longer. Rates are set in each Union Budget, so check the current year's rules or ask a tax professional. A later lesson covers records and filing.

## T+1 settlement

Indian equities settle **T+1**: shares and money move one working day after the trade day (T). If you buy on Monday, the shares arrive in your demat account on Tuesday.

> Net profit is profit after costs and tax. Judge every strategy on the net number.
