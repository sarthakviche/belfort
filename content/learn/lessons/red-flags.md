Most businesses with problems show them in the accounts before the share price fully does. Belfort's business check looks for the common warning signs and ranks each one as serious, moderate or minor.

## The serious ones

| Red flag | What Belfort looks for |
|---|---|
| A net loss | The latest year ended in a loss |
| Debt high and rising | Debt to equity above 1.5 and up by more than 0.3 in two years |
| Profit barely covers interest | Operating profit less than 1.5 times the interest bill |
| Profit not turning into cash | Profit reported, but cash from operations negative |

Any one of these makes the business read **cautious**.

## The moderate ones

- **Sales falling** more than 5% in a year.
- **Debt high**, above 1.5 times equity, even if it is not rising.
- **Interest cover thin**, between 1.5 and 3 times.
- **Operating margin down two years running.**
- **Money owed by customers growing faster than sales.** If receivables are a sizeable share of sales and grow 15 points faster than sales, the company may be booking sales it has not been paid for. Two years running, with large receivables, it counts as serious.
- **One-time gains lifted profit**: unusual items were more than 30% of pre-tax profit.

Three moderate concerns also make the business read cautious.

## Minor ones worth knowing

- **Dilution:** the share count rose more than 10%. New shares split the profit among more owners. Bonus issues and splits raise the count too, so check the reason.
- **Other income** above 40% of pre-tax profit: much of the profit comes from outside the main business.

## Promoter pledges

**Promoters** are the founders or controlling shareholders. A **pledge** means they have used their shares as security for a loan. If the share price falls, lenders can sell those shares, which can push the price down further. A high or rising pledge is a warning sign.

Belfort's data sources do not include pledge data, so the report always says the check did not run. To check yourself, open the company's latest shareholding pattern on the NSE or BSE website and look for shares pledged or encumbered by the promoter group.

## What a red flag does not mean

A concern is a reason to look closer, not proof of fraud or a forecast. Every finding names the figure and the year, so you can check it against the company's annual report.
