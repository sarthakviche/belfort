A **drawdown** is how far the account has fallen from its highest value. If it peaked at ₹2,20,000 and is now ₹1,98,000, the drawdown is 10%. Every trader has them. What matters is deciding in advance what you will do as one deepens, because in the middle of a losing run judgement is at its worst.

## Why rules beat willpower

After several losses, two urges pull in opposite directions: quit everything, or trade bigger to win it back. Both are reactions, not plans. A drawdown rule written while calm makes the decision for you.

## An example set of rules

| Drawdown from peak | Action |
|---|---|
| 5% | Review the last ten trades in the journal: plan followed? |
| 10% | Halve risk per trade, for example from 1% to 0.5% |
| 15% | Stop opening new trades for a week; review every rule |
| 20% | Stop live trading; go back to paper trading until the plan shows positive Average R again |

These numbers are examples, not a standard. Choose levels that fit your risk per trade: at 1% risk, ten straight losses are about a 10% drawdown, so a 10% trigger should fire on a bad streak, not on an ordinary week.

## Climbing back

Cutting risk in a drawdown slows the recovery, and that is the point. Smaller trades protect what is left while you find out whether the problem is the market, the plan or how you are following it. Return to normal risk only after a set condition, such as getting back halfway to the old peak.
