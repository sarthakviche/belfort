Tax time is far easier when your records are complete. This lesson is about what to keep, where it comes from and which return you will probably file.

> This is general education, not tax advice; check with a tax professional.

## What to keep

| Record | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Contract notes** | The bill your broker issues for each trading day: trades, prices, quantities and every charge | The proof of what you bought and sold, and of the charges |
| **Broker's tax P&L or capital gains statement** | A yearly summary of gains and losses, usually split short-term and long-term | The base for your return; check it, do not just trust it |
| **Annual statement of holdings and ledger** | Your demat holding statement and the broker's money ledger | Shows what you held and the cash that moved |
| **Your own trade log** | Date, share, quantity, price, reason, stop | Shows your decisions, not just the numbers |

On the income tax e-filing portal, your **Annual Information Statement (AIS)** lists share transactions reported about you. Compare it with your broker's statement before you file. If they differ, find out why.

## Your own log

Belfort's Practice journal is virtual, so those trades have no tax effect and are not a tax record. But the export in Settings (CSV or one JSON backup) is a good model of what a trade log should hold: dates, quantity, prices, your stop and your tag for the setup. When you trade for real, keep the same habit with real trades and real charges in a spreadsheet next to the broker's papers.

## Which return form?

Which form you file depends on the kind of income, so the right answer can change by person. In general:

- **Capital gains only, no business income** (delivery-based swing and positional trades): often **ITR-2**.
- **Business income** as well, for example from intraday or futures and options trading: **ITR-3**.

Use the form the Income Tax Department's e-filing site offers for your situation, or ask a tax professional.

## Intraday and F&O: business income (a mention)

Buying and selling the same share on the same day, without taking delivery, is generally treated as **speculative business income**. Futures and options are generally treated as **non-speculative business income**. They follow different rules from capital gains: how losses are set off, how long they carry forward, and whether the accounts need an audit. Belfort leaves futures and options out of its syllabus, so this lesson only tells you the category exists. If you do either, get professional help.

## How long to keep records

The tax department can look back at a return for a limited number of years, and the limit was changed by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, so check the current rule. Separately, an unused capital loss can be carried forward for 8 years, and you may need the papers behind it when you finally use it. A safe habit: keep everything for at least 8 years after the year of any loss you are carrying forward, and for the other years keep it as long as you can store it cheaply. Scans in a labelled folder are enough.
