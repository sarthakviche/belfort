objectives: Check whether an adviser or research analyst is registered with SEBI | Explain what registration does and does not tell you | Know where to complain: the firm, SCORES and the exchange

Anyone can say "I am SEBI registered". It takes two minutes to check. This lesson shows how, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## Who needs to be registered

In India, giving investment advice for a fee needs registration with SEBI as an **investment adviser (IA)**. Publishing research reports or recommendations on securities needs registration as a **research analyst (RA)**. A broker needs registration too. SEBI's registration numbers start with a prefix for the kind of registration; for example, research analysts' numbers begin with **INH** and investment advisers' with **INA**. A registered adviser or analyst should give you their number and show it in their communications. SEBI has also told registered firms not to work with unregistered people who give tips or claim returns.

## Check it in five steps

1. Ask for the person's or firm's **registration number** and exact name. If they refuse, that is your answer.
2. Open SEBI's website (sebi.gov.in), go to the list of **Registered Intermediaries** and choose the category: Investment Advisers, Research Analysts or Stock Brokers.
3. Search by the name or the registration number.
4. Check the **name, number, status and validity dates**. Look for any disciplinary action noted.
5. Make sure the details match what you were given: the same name, the same number, and the contact details you have. Fake certificates and borrowed numbers exist.

Do not use a link or phone number sent to you by the person you are checking. Type the SEBI address yourself.

SEBI also runs **SEBI Check**, a tool on its site and in its Saarthi app, to check whether a UPI handle or bank account belongs to a registered entity before you pay. Registered brokers and advisers collect UPI payments through handles that contain **@valid** (for example `name.brk@validbank`); a payment to any other handle is a warning sign. Never send money to a personal account for "advice" or "membership".

## What registration does not mean

A registration tells you the person is allowed to do the work and is answerable to SEBI. It does **not** mean SEBI endorses their views, and it does not assure any return. A registered adviser can be wrong and you can still lose money. Registration is a minimum, not a recommendation. The reverse is stronger: **an unregistered person selling tips is a red flag on its own.**

## If something goes wrong

1. **Complain to the firm first.** Write to the broker or adviser's grievance contact, say what happened, and keep a copy of everything: messages, contract notes, payment proof.
2. **File on SCORES**, SEBI's complaint system (scores.sebi.gov.in, also an app). You register with your details, and it is free. SEBI's guidance is to file within about one year of the event, and the firm is expected to reply in a set time (about 21 days). Track your complaint with its number.
3. **If your broker is the problem, complain to the exchange.** NSE takes complaints against its trading members, forwards them to the broker, and can send them on to a grievance committee and then arbitration. Its investor pages list the current steps.
4. SEBI also has an online dispute resolution platform (**SMART ODR**) for conciliation or arbitration when you are not satisfied with a firm's reply.

For a scam by someone who is not registered at all, also report it to the police, to the national cyber-crime portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or on the cyber-crime helpline **1930**, and tell SEBI through SCORES.

Sources: SEBI's registered-intermediaries search and SEBI Check pages; SEBI's SCORES 2.0 pages and FAQs; NSE investor charter and complaint pages; SEBI's 22 October 2026 press release. The SEBI and NSE sites could not be opened when this lesson was written, so time limits and steps come from summaries; read the current guidance on those sites before you file.
