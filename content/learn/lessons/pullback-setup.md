A **setup** is a repeatable reason to enter a trade, written down before you look at the money. The most common one for swing and positional traders is the **pullback**: a stock in an uptrend dips back towards support or its moving average, and you plan an entry there instead of chasing a high.

## The idea in one sentence

Join an existing uptrend at a calmer price, with a stop just below the level the dip came back to.

## What Belfort's rule checks

The research report runs the same checks on every stock. For a pullback, these matter most:

| Check | Swing style | Positional style |
|---|---|---|
| Trend is up | Price above the 50-day average, with higher highs and higher lows or the 20-day average above the 50-day | Price above the 200-day average, with higher highs and higher lows or the 50-day above the 200-day |
| Entry area | Within 1 ATR above the 20-day average, or within 1.5 ATR of support | Same, with the 50-day average |
| Not stretched | No more than 3 ATR above the fast average | Same |
| Stop distance | 8% or less below entry | 15% or less |
| Room to resistance | Next resistance at least 1.5 times the stop distance away | Same |

**ATR** (average true range) is the stock's normal daily movement in rupees. Measuring distances in ATR means the same rule works on a calm stock and a jumpy one.

## Putting a plan together

1. **Trend:** check the chart makes higher highs and higher lows. No uptrend, no pullback setup.
2. **Level:** find the support or average the price is dipping towards.
3. **Stop:** below that level, where the idea is clearly wrong if price gets there. Belfort suggests one half an ATR below the nearest support.
4. **Room:** measure from entry to the next resistance. If it is less than 1.5 times the stop distance, the reward on offer is small for the risk.
5. **Size:** risk 1% of capital using the position-size formula.

## A worked example

- An uptrend; the 20-day average is at ₹400 and ATR is ₹8.
- Price dips to ₹405, within 1 ATR of the average.
- Support at ₹392, stop half an ATR below at ₹388: a stop distance of ₹17, about 4%.
- Next resistance at ₹440: ₹35 away, about 2 times the stop distance.

Every price check passes. If the business and results checks also pass, the report says "Conditions met". That describes the chart today. It does not say the trade will work; many pullbacks keep falling through the stop, and that is what the stop is for.

## Common mistakes

- Buying every red day in a stock that is no longer making higher lows.
- Moving the stop lower when price approaches it.
- Entering far above the level, which makes the stop wide and the size small.

Look at this past chart. First decide whether it shows an uptrend at all: are the highs and lows rising? If not, the pullback setup does not apply, however attractive a dip looks. If it does, mark the dips that held above an earlier low and think about where a stop would have gone.
