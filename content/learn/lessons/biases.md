Most trading losses that hurt badly do not come from bad analysis. They come from good plans broken in the moment. Knowing the usual traps by name makes them easier to spot.

## The common ones

| Bias | What it feels like | What it does | Journal tag it often shows up as |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOMO (fear of missing out) | "It is running without me" | Buying far above the planned entry | Chased the entry |
| Revenge trading | "I need to win that back today" | Bigger or unplanned trades after a loss | Sized too big, No plan written |
| Overconfidence | "I am on a streak" | Raising risk after wins, skipping checks | Sized too big |
| Loss aversion | "It will come back" | Moving the stop lower to avoid taking a loss | Moved the stop |
| Fear of giving back gains | "Take it before it disappears" | Selling winners at the first wobble | Exited early |
| Confirmation bias | "Everything I read agrees with me" | Ignoring the report's unmet checks | No plan written |

## Why they are so strong

A loss hurts more than an equal gain pleases, so the mind works hard to avoid realising one. A run of wins feels like skill, even when it is ordinary variation. And a stock rising fast creates real discomfort in anyone not holding it. These are normal human reactions, which is exactly why they need a system rather than willpower.

## A check before every order

Before you place an order, answer four questions honestly:

1. Is this trade in my written plan, with a stop and a reason?
2. Is the size the one the formula gives, at my normal risk?
3. Am I entering near the planned level, not chasing?
4. Would I take this trade if my last trade had gone the other way?

If any answer is no, wait a day. A good setup is usually still there tomorrow; a bad decision cannot be undone.

## Measure, then fix

Tag mistakes honestly in the Practice journal. Once a tag has at least 3 trades, the Your journal panel shows what it has cost you in R. Seeing "Chased the entry: −0.7R over 6 trades" is more persuasive than any lesson.
