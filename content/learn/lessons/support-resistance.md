**Support** is a price where buyers stepped in before: falls stopped there and reversed. **Resistance** is a price where sellers stepped in: rallies stopped there. Traders remember these prices, and orders tend to gather around them.

## How to find them

1. Mark the swing lows and swing highs over the last year.
2. Look for prices that were touched more than once. Two or three touches make a level worth noting.
3. Treat each level as a **zone** of about one daily range (ATR), not an exact rupee figure. Price rarely turns at the same paisa twice.

Belfort's research report lists the nearest supports and resistances with their touch counts and the dates of the swings behind them.

## Why levels matter for a trade

Levels turn a vague idea into a plan with numbers:

| Part of the plan | Where levels help |
|---|---|
| Entry | Near support in an uptrend, the stop can sit close, so the risk is small |
| Stop-loss | Just below the support zone: if price breaks it, the reason for the trade is gone |
| Target | Near the next resistance, where sellers appeared before |
| Reward to risk | Distance to the target divided by the distance to the stop. Belfort's rule wants at least 1.5 |

## When levels break

When resistance breaks on strong volume, it often becomes support later. When support breaks, it often becomes resistance. A broken level is information: the people who defended it last time did not this time.

> Levels describe where buyers and sellers acted in the past. They are not promises about the future, so every plan still needs a stop.
