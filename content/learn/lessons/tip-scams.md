The fastest way to lose money in the market is to follow someone else's "sure thing". This lesson shows how the most common trap is built, so you can see it from the inside.

## How a pump-and-dump works

Many cases follow the same pattern:

1. **Pick a thin stock.** Small, lightly traded shares are easy to push around.
2. **Build a following.** A Telegram channel or WhatsApp group offers tips, often called "free education", and grows to thousands of members.
3. **Buy quietly.** The people running it accumulate the share before anyone is told.
4. **Announce the tip.** Members are urged to buy at once, with talk of a big move coming. Buying from many members lifts the price and the volume.
5. **Sell into the rush.** The organisers sell to the new buyers at the higher price.
6. **Price falls.** With no one buying, the price drops. Members hold shares worth far less than they paid.

The organisers' profit comes from the members' loss. In this pattern the tip is not analysis; it is the product they are selling to create demand.

## What SEBI has done

SEBI has acted on cases like this. In an interim order reported in January 2022, SEBI barred six people from dealing in securities and froze about ₹2.84 crore in their accounts. They ran a Telegram channel with about 52,000 subscribers that posted trading calls while claiming to be research analysts, without being registered. SEBI said the stock's price and volume rose after each recommendation and the organisers then sold. In May 2026 SEBI passed an interim order against seven people accused of pushing SME-listed stocks over X, Telegram and WhatsApp, impounding about ₹20 crore of alleged gains. Interim orders are early findings: the people named can respond, and the final outcome can differ.

In October 2026 SEBI warned about invitation-only WhatsApp groups. It said such groups often run on overseas numbers and present tips as free investor education, and that some members may be working with the operators to post false profit claims and praise, which it called "astroturfing". If a group is shut down, members may be invited to a new one by a new number; SEBI advised treating that as a red flag.

## Red flags

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Returns promised in advance, or "no loss" claims | No honest provider can know the future price |
| Urgency: "buy before 3 pm", "last chance" | Pressure removes your time to check |
| Screenshots of profits | Easy to fake, and they only show winners |
| "Operator" stories: a big player will push this stock | A story to make you buy; you cannot check it |
| Paid VIP groups after a free one | The free tips are bait for the paid product |
| The adviser is not on SEBI's register | Giving advice for a fee needs registration (next lesson) |
| Praise and trade confirmations from other members | These can be planted |
| An app or link you were sent to open an account or send money | Fake trading apps show invented profits until you try to withdraw |

Tip Check in Belfort checks a tip for these red flags. One red flag is a reason to stop. Several together are a pattern.

## What to do instead

- Do not join invitation-only groups run by strangers, and do not share your account details or send money to a tip provider.
- Use only your broker's app, installed from the official store and linked from the broker's own website.
- Check the registration of anyone offering advice (see the next lesson).
- Treat any tip as an unverified claim. Ask: what is the plan, where is the stop, how big can the loss be? If they cannot answer, the tip is not a trade.
