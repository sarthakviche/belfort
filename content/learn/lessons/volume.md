title: Volume: is anyone behind the move?
minutes: 5
objectives: Explain what daily volume measures | Compare a day's volume with its 20-day average | Use volume to judge breakouts and quiet pullbacks
---
**Volume** is the number of shares that changed hands in a session. Every share bought was sold by someone else, so volume does not say who won. It says how many people took part.

## Compare with normal, not with other stocks

Ten lakh shares is a quiet day for a large bank and a frantic day for a small company. So compare a day's volume with the stock's own **average volume**, usually over the last 20 sessions:

| Day's volume vs 20-day average | Rough reading |
|---|---|
| Below 0.7 times | Quiet: few people care today |
| Around 1 times | Normal |
| 1.5 times or more | Heavy: many people acted |

## Where volume helps a trade plan

- **Breakouts:** a close above resistance on heavy volume shows many buyers acted at the new price. Belfort's breakout check wants volume at least 1.5 times the 20-day average. A breakout on quiet volume fails more often.
- **Pullbacks:** in an uptrend, a dip on quiet volume suggests sellers are not in a hurry. A sharp fall on heavy volume deserves more caution.
- **Delivery:** Belfort's report also shows the delivery percentage from NSE's files: the share of traded shares actually taken into accounts rather than traded within the day.

Volume is a clue about participation, not a signal on its own. Use it to check whether a level or a breakout has people behind it.
