title: What to write before and after every trade
minutes: 6
objectives: Write a trade plan in a few lines before entering | Review a closed trade against its plan, not its result | Use the setup and mistake tags in Belfort's Practice journal
---
Memory flatters. Winning trades feel like skill and losing ones like bad luck. A **trade journal** is the cure: a few lines written before the trade and a few after, so you can later see what you actually did.

## Before the trade

Write the plan while you are calm, before any money is at risk. Belfort's Practice order form asks for most of it:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Setup | Pullback |
| Why this trade? | Pullback to the 20-day average in an uptrend; stop below the last swing low |
| Entry and stop | ₹412, stop ₹396 |
| Target, if any | ₹445, the next resistance |
| Risk | 1% of capital |

If you cannot fill in the stop or the reason, you do not have a trade yet.

## After the trade

Judge the **decision**, not the result. A trade that followed its plan and lost 1R was a good trade. A trade that broke the plan and made money was a lucky one, and luck does not repeat on purpose. In the review box, answer two questions:

1. Did I follow my plan?
2. What would I do the same, or differently, next time?

Then tag any mistakes Belfort lists: moved the stop, no plan written, chased the entry, sized too big, exited early.

## What the tags give you

Once a setup, holding time or mistake has at least 3 closed trades, the **Your journal** panel shows its win rate and average R, and lists what has worked and what has cost you. You might find that breakouts average +0.6R while trades where you chased the entry average −0.8R. That is the kind of finding a journal exists for, and it comes from your own trades.

## Keep it short

A journal you keep beats a detailed one you abandon. Two lines before and two after is enough, every time.
