title: Sector strength and rotation
minutes: 7
objectives: Explain why stocks in one sector tend to move together | Compare a sector's recent move with the index | Use sector strength as a filter, not a reason to trade
---
Banks react to interest rates, IT companies to the rupee and overseas demand, metal makers to global commodity prices. Because companies in a sector share the same drivers, their shares often move together. A stock's chart is partly its own story and partly its sector's.

## Measuring sector strength

Pick a few large companies in a sector, or the sector index NSE publishes (such as Nifty Bank or Nifty IT), and compare its move with the Nifty 50 over the same period:

| Sector over 3 months | Nifty 50 over 3 months | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| +9% | +3% | Stronger than the market |
| +2% | +3% | In line |
| −6% | +3% | Weaker than the market |

This is **relative strength**: how a sector or stock has done compared with the market. It is about the past few months only.

## Rotation

Money moves between sectors as conditions change. Over months, leadership can pass from one group to another. You do not need to predict rotation; you only need to notice which sectors are strong and which are weak today.

## Using it as a filter

- A pullback setup in a stock whose sector is stronger than the market has a tailwind behind it.
- The same setup in a sector falling hard is swimming against the current, so many traders skip it or take less risk.
- Several open trades in one sector are closer to one big trade: when the sector turns, they tend to fall together. The lesson on open risk covers this.

## A caution

A strong sector can turn quickly, and a weak one can stay weak far longer than expected. Relative strength describes recent months; it does not promise the next ones. Use it to choose which setups to study, then plan the trade with a stop as always.
