A trader holding for two weeks does not need a full company valuation. But a good chart on a weak business fails more often, so it is worth ten minutes to check that the business is not in trouble. Five numbers from the yearly accounts do most of the work, and the research report shows each one for up to five years.

## The five numbers

| Number | How it is worked out | What Belfort treats as strong | What it treats as weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales growth | This year's sales against last year's | Average above 10% a year, and still growing | Sales falling more than 5% |
| Operating margin | Operating profit ÷ sales | Up by 1.5 points or more on last year | Down two years running, by 3 points or more in total |
| Return on equity (ROE) | Net profit ÷ shareholders' money in the business | Above 15% | Below 8% |
| Debt to equity | Total debt ÷ shareholders' money | Below 0.5 | Above 1.5 |
| Cash from operations ÷ profit | Cash the business actually collected ÷ reported profit | 80% or more | Below 50% and weak for years, or negative |

## Why these five

- **Sales growth** shows whether customers want more of what the company sells.
- **Operating margin** shows whether it keeps more of each rupee of sales, or whether costs are eating it.
- **ROE** shows how well it uses the money shareholders have left in it.
- **Debt to equity** shows how much it relies on borrowing. High debt turns a bad year into a dangerous one.
- **Cash from operations** checks that profit is real. Profit is an accounting figure; cash in the bank is harder to dress up.

## Reading them together

One number rarely tells the story. Fast sales growth with a falling margin can mean the company is buying sales with discounts. A high ROE with high debt can come from borrowing rather than a good business. Look for numbers that agree with each other.

The report adds its findings up. Any serious concern, or three moderate ones, makes the business read **cautious**; few concerns and three or more strengths make it **constructive**; anything in between is **neutral**. These describe the accounts, not where the share price goes next.

## Banks and lenders are different

For banks and finance companies, borrowing is the raw material, so debt, margin and cash-flow checks mean something different. Belfort skips them for lenders and says so. The quality of their loans matters most, and that is not in this data source.

## Where the figures come from

The report uses yearly statements from Yahoo Finance and labels each figure with its financial year. If the source does not return a number, the report shows it as missing rather than guessing.
