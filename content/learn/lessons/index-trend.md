Most stocks move with the market. When the Nifty 50 is falling, even good setups in individual stocks fail more often, because sellers are everywhere. Checking the market first takes a minute and puts every stock chart in context.

## The index trend

Read the Nifty 50 chart with the same rule you use for a stock:

- **Uptrend:** higher highs and higher lows, price above its 50-day and 200-day averages.
- **Downtrend:** lower highs and lower lows, price below falling averages.
- **Sideways:** neither; the index chops inside a range.

An index is an average of many companies, so its trend changes slowly. That makes it a useful backdrop rather than a trading signal.

## Breadth: how many stocks are taking part

The index can rise while most stocks fall, if a few heavyweights carry it. **Breadth** measures participation. Two simple versions:

| Measure | How it works | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Share above the 50-day average | Count the stocks in a group trading above their own 50-day average | Above about 60%: broad strength. Below about 40%: broad weakness |
| Advances against declines | Count stocks that closed up against stocks that closed down today | Many more advances than declines, day after day, shows wide buying |

When the index makes a new high but fewer stocks are above their averages each week, the rise is getting narrower. That is worth noticing, not a forecast.

## Using the backdrop

The market's trend does not decide a single trade, but it can decide how many you take:

- **Strong index and broad participation:** your normal number of trades and normal risk.
- **Weak index or narrow participation:** fewer new trades, smaller risk, or simply watching.
- **Falling index with poor breadth:** many traders take no new long trades at all.

Write your choice into your rules before the market forces it on you.
